Deep dive — Gaztransport & Technigaz (GTT.PA) Add

Follow-up to the review of 25 Nov 2026, the one “Add” verdict: the French designer of the membrane containment systems inside LNG carriers. Why it earns the verdict, what the risks are, and the estimated odds for 2027.

The business in one minute

GTT licenses its patented containment technology to shipyards and assists them through construction. 86% of the world's large LNG-carrier fleet — and 100% of large-carrier orders since 2015 — uses GTT systems, yet a containment system is only ~3% of a ~US$250m ship: too cheap for customers to fight over, and priced for 67–68% EBITDA margins at GTT. Orders convert into revenue with a lag (~9–12 months to engineering start, then ~18 months of construction), which is why the 2022 order boom produced record 2024–25 results, and 2025's weak intake produces the 2026 dip. Contracts are euro-denominated — no material FX exposure.

Backlog: 299 units / €1.8bn (30 Sep 2026), rebuilt from the €1.59bn trough at end-2025. €655m of 2027 revenue is already secured — ~80% of consensus; 2028 visibility €603m. 2026 is already the second-best intake year ever, and further Q4 orders keep landing.

2027 — the swing factor is new orders

What matters next year is not the backlog but order intake, which follows liquefaction projects reaching final investment decision (FID). 2025 saw a record 84 Mtpa of FIDs and 2026 has added ~51 Mtpa; most of the ships those projects need are still un-ordered. Projects explicitly targeting 2027 decisions: Qatar North Field West (16 Mtpa), LNG Canada Phase 2 (14 Mtpa), Cheniere Sabine Pass Train 7, Venture Global CP2 Phase 3, Alaska LNG, ST LNG Matagorda. Independent forecasts cluster at ~70 LNGC orders in 2027 (Clarksons ~70; Goldman ~86/yr; UBS ~66/yr; GTT's own long-term ~55/yr) — comfortably above 2025's 45. Counter-currents: ~US$250m newbuild prices, a 2027 delivery wave (91–95 ships) and spot charter rates near US$20,000/day.

The moat — and the two threats

  • Moat: 575 of 575 large LNG carriers ordered 2016–2025 use GTT; net cash €295m; 80% payout; five consecutive dividend increases; the Korean KC-1 alternative failed commercially (court ordered KOGAS to reimburse Samsung Heavy ~KRW 300bn, Jan 2026).
  • Threat 1 — Korea's state push: a KOGAS tender for one KC-2-equipped 174,000 m³ carrier (bids due 31 Dec 2026, sea trials 2028, export ambitions by 2030). Not a 2027 earnings threat, but the first credible attempt to break the monopoly.
  • Threat 2 — Chinese substitution: five Chinese yards build LNG carriers (25% of GTT's revenue) and domestic designs exist, but no commercial large-carrier order has used a non-GTT system in 2025–26. Real, but long-dated.

Valuation

15.2x trailing earnings (~15x 2027 consensus) against a 2022–26 range of 13.7x (2024 trough) to 28.8x — mid-range, not a bargain. Dividend €8.94 for FY2025 (+19%, 80% payout; FY2026 interim €4.30, ex-date 8 Dec), ~5% yield. French source withholding on dividends is ~12.8%. Consensus (12 analysts, 25 Nov 2026): Strong Buy, average target €233.83 (range €184–275) — analyst opinions, reported as facts.

Deep dive — Care Property Invest (CPINV.BR) Keep

Follow-up to the review of 25 Nov 2026: Belgium’s pure-play healthcare REIT — 158 care projects, €1.38bn fair value, four countries. Why the verdict is “keep, do not add”, and what the 7.8% yield is really paying for.

The business in one minute

CPINV owns and lets care homes and assisted-living property (Belgium 68%, Netherlands 17%, Spain 8%, Ireland 7% of fair value) on 20-year+ triple-net leases to care operators — Korian Belgium is the largest, with 17 projects after the December 2025 Welfare Estates deal. Leases are indexed annually (2.44% average in 2026) and contractual occupancy is 100%; operator fees are largely publicly funded, so the rent stream sits close to government risk. A legacy Belgian municipal/CPAS lease book (~25% of rent) expires gradually from 2026 — the Korian deal was bought, in management’s words, to offset exactly that decline. The operating margin excluding non-cash items is ~88%.

The balance sheet — the story that matters

  • Debt ratio 49.05% against the 50% legal ceiling for Belgian REITs (EPRA LTV 47.6%, net debt/EBITDA 9.3×) — about €26m of headroom, the weakest sheet among the Belgian REITs covered in the review.
  • ~93.5% of debt hedged at an average cost of 3.11% — comfortable today, but the hedges roll over in 2027–28 into higher rates (Belgian 10-year ~4.3%).
  • In Q3 2026, part of maturing commercial paper could not be refinanced and backup credit lines were drawn; €85.6m remained available at 30 Sep, and an ABN AMRO €60m facility was extended to 2029.
  • A €55.5m rights issue at €10.50 in December 2025 diluted EPS by ~€0.14 — 9M 2026 EPRA earnings per share fell 2.3% while total earnings rose 11.6%. The company says a further increase is “not excluded” to stay under the 50% ceiling.

The two risks that matter

  • Refinancing / dilution: every 2027 refinancing reprices hedges upward, and any new acquisition or squeeze at a 49% debt ratio raises the rights-issue question. The 25% discount to NTA is the market pricing exactly this.
  • Operator counterparty risk: the January 2026 failure of Belgian operator Apricusa touched three sites (~6% of contractual rents; ~−€0.02 EPS, cushioned by six-month bank guarantees). Resolved — the sites were re-housed with operator Hestia — but the sector pattern is real: rent collection dipped from 98% to 96% in Q1 2026. Renegotiations with Armonea, Ricusa and Orélia are the current watch items.

Valuation

0.75× EPRA NTA (€12.88 vs €17.12) — a ~25% discount; ~11× EPRA earnings (€1.16 trailing, ≥€1.13 guided for FY2026, raised twice, last on 4 Nov). Dividend €1.00 gross / €0.85 net (15% GVV withholding, not the 30% on ordinary Belgian dividends) — unchanged since FY2022, ~88% of guided EPRA EPS against the company’s own 80–85% long-term payout target. Gross yield 7.8% (net 6.6%), annual payment. Consensus (5 analysts, 25 Nov 2026): Hold, average target €13.42 (range ~€10.50–16.00): KBC Securities Hold €13.50 (5 Nov), Berenberg Buy €16 (11 Oct), Kepler Cheuvreux Hold €13.10 (5 Jun) — analyst opinions, reported as facts. The average target is only ~4% above the price: the analysts are not betting on a re-rating either.

Deep dive — Klépierre (LI.PA), the CPINV alternative Add

The CPINV dive ended with “do not add” on balance-sheet grounds. This one screens the European REIT market for the alternative, and lands on Klépierre — the mirror image of CPINV’s problem: a 33.8% LTV, A−/A-rated pan-European fortress paying a growing 5% dividend.

The business in one minute

Europe’s second-largest listed mall owner: ~70 dominant centres, €21.2bn value, 10+ countries (France 38%, Southern Europe 36%, Scandinavia 12%, rest of Europe 14% of retailer sales). Simon Property Group holds ~22% and has sat on the board since 2012. Income comes from indexed leases plus turnover top-ups: financial occupancy 97.1%, retailer sales +3.9% like-for-like in H1 2026, renewal uplifts +5.0%, occupancy-cost ratio 12.5%. Indexation has faded (1.8% → ~0.8% in 2026) — uplifts and occupancy now carry the growth, and H1 2026 EPRA NTA rose +5.3% in six months.

The balance sheet — the opposite of CPINV’s problem

  • LTV 33.8% (vs CPINV 49.05%): no regulatory-ceiling pressure, no forced equity, no dilution risk.
  • A− (S&P) / A (Fitch); net debt/EBITDA 6.6× (historic low); interest cover 7.2×.
  • Cost of debt 1.9%, average maturity 6.1 years — the 2027–28 refinancing wave that threatens the Belgian REITs is a non-event here.
  • €2.4bn liquidity; €205m disposals and a €160m acquisition in 2025 show capital recycling in both directions.

The two risks that matter

  • The consumer, not the internet: a 2027 European downturn (France softness, Germany already the laggard) would stall uplifts and pressure occupancy — every −1pp on retailer sales hits rents first, NAV second. E-commerce is the narrative; discretionary spending is the income risk.
  • You buy at fair value: ~1.0× NTA — the discount closed over the past year (52-week low was ~0.82× NTA). No cushion: the growth has to arrive. (Consensus target €37.94 sits exactly on the price.)

Valuation

€38.02 vs EPRA NTA €37.80 = ~1.01× NAV; ~14× FY2026E net current cash flow (guided €2.77–2.80/share, raised twice). Dividend €1.90 for FY2025 (~70% payout), paid Mar + Jul, ~5.0% yield; FY2026E €1.96 (+3.1%), and the payout has grown ~3%/yr since the 2020 COVID cut (€2.20 → €1.00 → €1.70 → … → €1.90). French SIIC withholding ~12.8%, credited against the Belgian ~30% — the same mechanism as GTT. Consensus (14 analysts, 25 Nov 2026): Hold, average target €37.94 (range €31–44.20) — analyst opinions, reported as facts.
