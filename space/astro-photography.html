Astro Photography

Shooting the Moon and the planets with two of my own rigs — a 2007 Nikon D300 with a 300 mm lens, and the Bresser MC-152 telescope with the SVBONY SV705C camera — and one shared software workflow that turns blurry frames into sharp pictures.

My own astrophotography project, started 15 November 2026. The two hardware setups, side by side: a Nikon D300 (12.3 MP DX sensor) with an AF-S VR Zoom-Nikkor 70–300 mm f/4.5–5.6G, and the Bresser Messier MC-152/2250 Maksutov telescope with the SVBONY SV705C planetary camera (Sony IMX585). Both shoot the same sky — but they capture it so differently that they need two workflows. Those workflows, and the one shared software stack, are explained here.

Why two workflows?

The D300 is a photo camera: it takes individual stills, cannot record video, and has a small buffer. The SV705C is a video camera built for lucky imaging. The D300 therefore needs preparation steps (PIPP, NX Studio, FastStone) that the video camera makes unnecessary — and from stacking onward both rigs use exactly the same programs. The step-by-step software handling for both lives in one place: the Software How-To.

⏰ Right now (15–19 November 2026)

Two perfect targets: the waxing Moon heading to first quarter (17 Nov) — craters in long shadows along the terminator — and Saturn, this autumn’s evening planet, high in the south. You can shoot both tonight, with either rig.
