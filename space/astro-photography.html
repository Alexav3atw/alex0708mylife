Astro Photography

Shooting the Moon and the planets with a 2007 Nikon D300 and a 300 mm lens — every setting, the exposure tables, and the free stacking workflow that turns 100 blurry frames into one sharp picture.

This is my own astrophotography project, started 15 November 2026. The gear: a Nikon D300 (12.3 MP DX sensor) with an AF-S VR Zoom-Nikkor 70–300 mm f/4.5–5.6G. The targets: the Moon and the planets. Everything below was researched and verified against the official Nikon manual and current sources — including what 300 mm on a DX sensor can and cannot do.

Pixel scale at 300 mm
3.78″/px
Every pixel covers 3.78 arcseconds of sky — the true “magnification” you have
Moon on the sensor
±490 px
About one sixth of the frame height — a detailed Moon ball, not a filled frame
Saturn with rings
±11 px
A tiny oval with a ring — small but real, and stacking makes it shine
Jupiter’s disc
±12 px
Plus its four Galilean moons as dots in a row — visible on every good frame
⏰ Right now (15–19 November 2026)

Two perfect targets: the waxing Moon heading to first quarter (17 Nov) — craters in long shadows along the terminator — and Saturn, this autumn’s evening planet, high in the south. You can shoot both tonight.
