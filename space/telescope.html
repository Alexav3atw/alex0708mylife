Telescope

My own telescope: a Bresser Messier MC-152/2250 Maksutov on an EXOS-2 PMC-Eight GoTo mount — what it can show, the SVBONY SV705C camera setup, and how to get ready for a night of imaging. The complete imaging workflow lives in the Astro Photography subject.

My telescope project, started 15 November 2026. The gear: a BRESSER Messier MC-152/2250 EXOS-2 PMC-Eight GoTo — a 152 mm Maksutov-Cassegrain (2250 mm focal length, f/14.8) on a German equatorial mount with Wi-Fi GoTo control — plus the SVBONY SV705C planetary camera (Sony IMX585). What you can see

The Moon & the planets — the sweet spot

The long 2250 mm focal length and a colour-free Mak design deliver extremely sharp, high-contrast views:

  • Moon: crater details, mountain ranges, rilles and shadow lines along the terminator.
  • Jupiter: cloud belts, the Great Red Spot, and the shadow transits of the Galilean moons.
  • Saturn: ring structure (the Cassini division on steady nights) and bright moons like Titan.
  • Mars & Venus: polar caps and phase changes.

Double stars & globular clusters

The 0.91″ resolving power splits close double stars and resolves individual stars in compact globular clusters like M13 and M3 — impressive through any eyepiece.

Deep sky — the honest limit

Compact, bright objects (the Ring Nebula M57, the Dumbbell M27, the core of the Orion Nebula M42) are beautiful. Large nebulae and open clusters like the Pleiades do not fit the narrow field of view, and the slow f/14.8 optics make faint extended nebulae a long-exposure project — that is where the Software How-To comes in.

The mount & the reviews

The EXOS-2 German equatorial mount carries the tube vibration-free (13 kg payload), and the open-source PMC-Eight GoTo system drives both axes over Wi-Fi or USB — ASCOM-compatible, with an ST-4 autoguider port, and controlled by the free ExploreStars app. No hand controller is included; a tablet, smartphone or laptop is the remote. An illuminated polar finder is pre-installed for polar alignment.

The imaging setup: SVBONY SV705C

For Moon and planet photography the choice is the SVBONY SV705C, built around the modern Sony IMX585 (STARVIS 2) sensor. Why this combination matches optically:

  • Perfect sampling: the Nyquist rule says a 2.9 µm pixel wants about f/14.5 (5 × 2.9) — the MC-152 is f/14.8. You get the telescope’s full optical resolution at prime focus, with no Barlow lens and no light loss.
  • A big 4K field (11.2 × 6.3 mm): four times the sensor area of entry-level planetary cameras (like the IMX662) — large parts of the lunar surface fit in one frame and planets stay easy to keep in view. At 0.27″/px the sensor covers 1026 × 580″ (17.1′ × 9.7′), so the whole Moon is a mosaic of about 9–12 panes — the workflow is in the Software How-To — but every detail you capture is fully resolved.
  • A UV/IR-cut filter is mandatory: the SV705C has a clear AR-coated window that also passes infrared — which focuses at a different point and wrecks colours. A 1.25″ UV/IR-cut filter restores sharp detail and natural colour.
  • 1:8 microfocuser reduction: at 2250 mm the focus zone is razor-thin. The Bresser 1:8 gear set on the Hexafoc focuser makes fine focusing possible without touching the tube.

Getting ready to image

The full capture and processing workflow lives in the Software How-To (Astro Photography subject); this is the physical preparation that happens outside, before the laptop opens.

  1. Cooldown (60–90 minutes): put the telescope outside so the mirror and the thick meniscus lens reach outside temperature. Fit a dew shield on the front.
  2. Polar alignment & GoTo: level the tripod with the north leg pointing north, roughly align the EXOS-2 on Polaris, then run a 2-star or 3-star alignment in the ExploreStars app.
  3. Centre visually: find your target (Jupiter, or a lunar crater) with the included 26 mm eyepiece and the star diagonal, and put it exactly in the middle of the field.
  4. Mount the camera (prime focus): remove the eyepiece and the 90° star diagonal. Leave the included Hexafoc extension tube(s) between the tube and the focuser in place — they set the back-focus distance for camera use; without them the camera may not reach focus. Screw the 1.25″ UV/IR-cut filter onto the nose piece of the SV705C, slide the camera straight into the 1.25″ holder of the focuser, and connect it to a blue USB 3.0 port of your laptop.

Then open SharpCap and follow the Workflow B steps in the Software How-To.
