The Software How-To

One page, two rigs, one shared stack: capture and post-process Moon and planet photos from the Nikon D300 and the Bresser MC-152 + SVBONY SV705C — every program, click by click. All free.

Last verified 15 November 2026. This is the only place on this site with the step-by-step software handling — for both hardware setups. The camera settings for the D300 live in the complete D300 guide; the telescope itself and its physical preparation live on the Telescope page.

The two pipelines at a glance

StepD300 + 300 mm lensMC-152 + SV705C
1. CaptureOn the camera: stills (NEF for the Moon, JPEG bursts for planets)SharpCap: SER video, ROI, 45–250 fps
2. PrepFastStone cull · NX Studio (single shots) · PIPP converts, centres and sorts the stillsNone needed — the SER file goes straight to the stacker
3. StackAutoStakkert!4 (shared — one set of steps, settings per rig)
4. SharpenRegiStax 6 wavelets (shared)
5. PolishNX Studio or GIMPGIMP (optional)
Why two workflows?

The D300 (2007) is a photo camera: it takes individual stills and cannot record video, and its buffer is small. The SV705C is a video camera built for lucky imaging: thousands of frames in one SER file. So the D300 needs preparation steps (PIPP, NX Studio, FastStone) that turn stills into a video-like stream — the video camera skips all of that. From stacking onward both rigs use exactly the same programs and the same steps; only the parameter values differ, because the two rigs see the sky at very different pixel scales.

Workflow A — the D300 + 300 mm lens

You have shot your frames (settings and shooting plans are in the complete D300 guide). These are the D300-specific preparation steps — after PIPP, continue with the shared stack below.

A1 — getting the photos off the camera

  1. Take the CF card out of the camera, put it in a card reader, and plug it into the PC.
  2. Create a dated folder and copy everything there, e.g. fotos\maan\RAW\2026-11-17\. Never edit files on the card itself.
  3. Put the card back in the camera and format it there (camera format is the reliable one).

Culling with FastStone (5 minutes, saves trouble later)

  1. Open FastStone → browse to your RAW folder → double-click the first photo (full screen).
  2. Arrow keys to walk through the frames. Delete (or the red cross) removes hopeless frames: blown-out, half-out-of-frame, or visibly shaken.
  3. Keep the rest — PIPP and AutoStakkert do the fine sorting. For planets keep at least 40–60 frames; for the Moon 15–25 is plenty.

A2 — Nikon NX Studio: your NEF workbench

NX Studio is Nikon’s own free editor: it opens D300 NEF files natively and understands every in-camera setting (Picture Control, Active D-Lighting, white balance). Two jobs here: (A) making a single Moon shot look great, and (B) converting NEFs to TIFF when you want to bypass PIPP.

2A — One beautiful Moon shot (10 minutes)

  1. Launch NX Studio (sign in once with a free Nikon ID). The left panel is the folder browser — navigate to your RAW folder. Thumbnails appear in the middle.
  2. Double-click the sharpest NEF to open it in the editor. The adjustment panel sits on the right (toggle it with the sliders icon if hidden).
  3. Adjust, top to bottom:
    • Exposure Compensation: nudge until the Moon is bright but nowhere pure white (watch the histogram — no pile-up against the right edge).
    • White Balance: choose “Direct sunlight”, or manual around 5200 K.
    • Picture Control → Sharpening: +3 to +5 — this is where the craters pop. Clarity: +2 to +4.
    • Contrast: +10 to +20 for punch.
    • Noise Reduction: Low — at ISO 200 barely needed.
  4. File → Convert Files → format JPEG, quality highest → destination fotos\maan\bewerkt\ → Convert.

2B — Batch-converting NEF → TIFF (the PIPP alternative)

If you ever want to stack without PIPP: select all NEFs in the browser (Ctrl+A) → File → Convert Files → format TIFF (16-bit) → pick a new folder → Convert. The TIFFs go straight into AutoStakkert!. RawTherapee is a free alternative that applies identical neutral settings to a whole batch. (PIPP does this conversion + centring + cropping in one go, which is why the normal route goes through PIPP.)

A3 — PIPP: pre-processing

PIPP turns a pile of raw frames into a tidy, centred, sorted stream — ready for AutoStakkert!. It works as a left-to-right wizard with five tabs: Source Files → Processing Options → Quality Options → Output Options → Do Processing. It reads your NEF files directly and debayers them — but test your own NEFs once, because very old PIPP versions lacked NEF support; if yours refuses them, use the NX Studio batch (step 2B). PIPP also stretches the histogram (white point at its 75% default) and can calibrate with dark and flat frames.

3A — Planets (Saturn / Jupiter)

  1. Source Files: “Add Image Files” → select all frames (NEF or JPEG) of one planet, one evening. In “Optimise Options For” choose Planetary.
  2. Processing Options: make sure Frame Stabilisation Mode = Object/Planetary and Enable Object Detection is ticked. Click “Test Options” (bottom right): the preview must show a box around the planet in every test frame. If the box grabs a hot pixel instead of the planet, lower “Object Detection Threshold” (more sensitive) or raise “Minimum Object Size”.
    • Enable Cropping ticked, crop size 400 × 400 px — generously around the planet (it is only ±12 px, but the moons of Jupiter reach ±160 px out).
    • Leave Convert Colour to Monochrome unticked (you want colour).
    • Leave the debayer settings on their defaults (PIPP debayers your NEFs automatically).
  3. Quality Options: tick “Only Keep The Best Quality Frames” and keep the best 80–90% (PIPP does the coarse sort; AutoStakkert does the fine one).
  4. Output Options: format AVI with codec ULRG (lossless) — one tidy file. (A TIFF series or a single SER file work too, and a TIFF series is easier to inspect.) Tick “Use Source File Directory” so the output lands next to your frames with a _pipp suffix.
  5. Do Processing → Start Processing. Watch the progress bar; it ends with a success message and the output filename.

3B — The Moon

  1. Source Files: add your 15–25 Moon NEFs → “Optimise Options For” = Solar/Lunar Full Disc.
  2. Processing Options: object detection finds the disc automatically. Click “Test Options” and check that the crop box (roughly 600–900 px, your Moon is ±490 px) contains the whole disc with some margin. Convert Colour to Monochrome: unticked.
  3. Quality Options: keep the best 90%.
  4. Output Options: AVI (ULRG), a TIFF series or a single SER file — all fine.
  5. Do Processing → Start Processing.
PIPP produced 0 frames?

Object detection failed: click “Test Options” again and loosen the detection threshold, or untick “Enable Object Detection” and set the crop manually around where the Moon/planet sits.

That is the end of the D300-specific part. Continue with the shared stack: AutoStakkert!4 → RegiStax 6 → GIMP.

Workflow B — the telescope + SV705C

You have set up the telescope outside (cooldown, polar alignment, camera at prime focus — all on the Telescope page). These are the capture steps — after SharpCap, continue with the shared stack below.

B1 — capture with SharpCap

  1. Select the camera: top left, Cameras → SVBONY SV705C.
  2. Focus: the image probably starts black or as one big blurry disc. Set Exposure temporarily high (e.g. 50 ms) and Gain to 250. Turn the coarse focus knob until the disc is as small as possible, then use the 1:8 microfocuser for fine detail — craters on the Moon, cloud belts on Jupiter.
  3. Colour settings (debayer): in Camera Controls set Colour Space to RAW8 (small files, maximum speed) and Debayer Preview to On so you see colour on screen while focusing.
  4. Shrink the field (ROI) for planets:
    • Moon: full resolution (3840x2160).
    • Planets: a planet is a tiny dot on a 4K sensor. Set Capture Area to 640x480 or 800x600 — the frame rate jumps from 45 fps to 150–250 fps and the video files stay small.
  5. Exposure & gain (the most important step):
    • Open the Histogram (toolbar button at the top).
    • Set Exposure between 5 ms and 15 ms — a short shutter “freezes” the seeing.
    • Raise Gain until the histogram peak sits between 60% and 75% (never above 80%, or bright areas blow out).
    • White Balance: click Auto, or set Red/Blue manually so the colour curves in the histogram line up.
  6. Format & duration:
    • Output Format: always SER files (*.ser) — never compressed AVI/MP4.
    • Click Start Capture with a fixed duration:
    TargetDurationWhy
    Jupiter90–120 sJupiter spins on its axis in 10 hours — longer videos smear the detail
    Saturn / Mars180 s (3 min)Slower rotation — more frames, less noise
    Moon60–90 sAbout 2,000–3,000 frames — plenty for a sharp stack

That is the end of the telescope-specific part. Continue with the shared stack below.

The shared stack (both rigs)

From here on, both rigs run through exactly the same programs and the same steps. Where a setting depends on the rig, the table below gives both values — because the D300 sees a planet as ±12 px and the SV705C as ±170 px, a few numbers differ; the procedure does not.

S1 — AutoStakkert!4: stacking

AutoStakkert (AS!4) is the heart of the workflow: it measures the quality of every frame, picks the best ones, and averages them into one noise-free image. No installer — unzip and run AutoStakkert.exe.

  1. Open: button 1. Open → select your input: the PIPP output (D300: the _pipp.avi or the TIFF folder) or the SharpCap .ser video (telescope). The frames load in the top window.
  2. Image Stabilization: choose Surface for the Moon, Planet (COG) for planets. For a lunar Surface run, place the green anchor point with Ctrl + click on a contrast-rich crater rim.
  3. Alignment points (APs): see the settings table — AP size differs per rig, then click Place AP grid (for planets: click on the planet; right-click removes stray APs).
  4. Quality Estimator: leave on “Gradient” (size 4) and Noise Robust: 3 (use 4 for noisy Saturn bursts from the D300 at ISO 1600).
  5. Click “Analyse” → wait → the lower window now shows a green quality curve across all frames (the horizontal grid lines mark 25% steps). High green = sharp frame.
  6. Stack Options — per rig:

AP sizeMoon 48 px (auto grid) · planets 24 px on the planetMoon 64–104 px (up to 128 in poor seeing) · planets 48 px

  1. Click “Stack” → wait (the progress bar counts frames) → the result appears in an AS_... subfolder next to the source file: AS_..._stack.tif. Open that one in RegiStax — not any _conv variant.
AS!4 refuses your files?

You fed it NEF files directly — AS!4 cannot read NEF. Run them through PIPP first (or convert to 16-bit TIFF with NX Studio, see step 2B). SER files from SharpCap open directly.

S2 — RegiStax 6: wavelets (the sharpening)

RegiStax 6 is the classic wavelet sharpener. It is old (2011) but still the standard final step for Moon and planets — and it is the same tool for both rigs.

  1. Launch RegiStax → “Select” (top-left) → open your AS_..._stack.tif.
  2. Colour balance: on the right, RGB Balance → Auto Balance — any colour cast disappears.
  3. Optional for planets: if the disc has a red edge on one side and blue on the other, go to tab “RGB Align” → “Estimate” → “Do All” — the colours slide back together.
  4. Go to the “Wavelet” tab. Left side: 6 layers, finest (1) to coarsest (6).
    • Planets: raise layers 2–3 first (the disc’s detail lives there), layer 1 just a touch. Watch the preview at full size.
    • Moon: raise layers 1–3 gently — layers 1–2 carry the crater detail.
    • Per layer: set Denoise to about 0.10–0.30 (more if the image is noisy).
    • Leave the default scheme (“Default”/dyadic). Linear + Gaussian are fine alternatives, not requirements.
  5. How much is too much? Bright rings around craters or around the planet edge (“onion rings”) and a plastic look mean: too far. Back the layers off, raise denoise.
  6. Click “Do All” (applies the wavelets to the full image) → then “Save Image” (top bar) → save as TIFF or JPEG in fotos\...\gestackt\.
RegiStax says “invalid TIFF header”?

Known quirk with some TIFF variants. Open the file in GIMP → File → Export As… → PNG → open that PNG in RegiStax.

Alternatives for this sharpening step exist — ImPPG (Lucy-Richardson deconvolution) or Siril — but one sharpener is enough, and this site standardises on RegiStax 6. One nuance for telescope close-ups: oversampled detail (0.27″/px) responds more on wavelet layers 2–4, so keep layer 1 gentle there.

S3 — GIMP: the final polish

Optional but worth it, especially for planets. Four moves, in order:

  1. Colors → Levels: move the white/black triangles inward until the histogram touches the data — or try “Auto” and refine. This sets the brightness floor and ceiling (the background turns true black).
  2. Colors → Color Balance: Saturn likes a warm yellow push in the highlights; Jupiter a gentle warm cream. Subtle is enough (±10).
  3. Filters → Enhance → Sharpen (Unsharp Mask): radius 1–2 px, amount 0.3–0.8 — a whisper, not a shout (the wavelets already did the heavy lifting).
  4. Image → Scale Image (for sharing: 1200–1600 px wide is plenty) → File → Export As… → JPEG, quality 90 → fotos\...\bewerkt\.

Special case — the full-disc Moon mosaic

With the telescope the sensor covers 1026 × 580″ (17.1′ × 9.7′) while the Moon is about 31′ across — so a full disc is about 9 to 12 overlapping panes. The pane workflow:

  1. Plan the grid: panes with 20–25% overlap, so no crater falls between two panes.
  2. Capture each pane: RAW8 keeps the data volume manageable — a 12-bit mosaic approaches 250 GB, 8-bit halves that (use a fast SSD and a USB 3 port). Take 1,000–2,000 frames per pane, exposure by histogram (start around 2–8 ms, peak at 60–75%, no clipping). Record flat frames too — dust shadows show clearly on this sensor. Finish all panes within about 15–20 minutes: shadows at the terminator change quickly — and refocus between panes when the temperature is shifting.
  3. Stack each pane: AutoStakkert!4 → Surface · AP size 64–128 px (larger in poor seeing) · stack the best 10–25% · no drizzle · one 16-bit TIFF per pane.
  4. Stitch the panes (Hugin, PTGui or similar) — and only then sharpen the whole mosaic, so the sharpening is consistent across the seams. Oversampled telescope detail responds more on wavelet layers 2–4: keep layer 1 gentle. Use RGB Align to fix colour fringing from low altitude.
  5. Check the scale: a stitched full disc should measure close to 7,000 px across — if it is far off, a pane was skipped.

At f/14.8 the sampling is already dense (some guides call it slightly oversampled — the green-light Nyquist rule of thumb is about f/10.5 for 2.9 µm pixels), so drizzle and a Barlow lens add nothing here.

Three complete recipes, start to finish

Recipe A — The Moon with the D300 (target: 45 minutes)

  1. Copy the NEFs to fotos\maan\RAW\2026-11-17\.
  2. FastStone: delete the obviously bad frames, keep 20+.
  3. PIPP: Solar/Lunar Full Disc · colour · keep best 90% · output AVI (ULRG).
  4. AutoStakkert!4: Surface · AP 48 px auto grid · Noise Robust 3 · Analyse · stack best 50% · drizzle off · 16-bit TIFF.
  5. RegiStax 6: Wavelet layers 1–3 gently + denoise 0.10–0.30 · Do All · save TIFF to fotos\maan\gestackt\.
  6. NX Studio (step 2A): exposure, white balance 5200 K, sharpening +3…5, clarity +2…4, contrast +15 · Convert Files → JPEG → fotos\maan\bewerkt\maan-2026-11-17.jpg. Done 🌙

Recipe B — Saturn with the D300, this evening (target: 60 minutes)

  1. Copy the JPEG bursts to fotos\planeten\RAW\2026-11-17-saturnus\.
  2. FastStone: keep the best 60–80 frames of your series.
  3. PIPP: Planetary · centre + crop 400×400 · keep best 80% · output AVI (ULRG).
  4. AutoStakkert!4: Planet (COG) · AP 24 px on the planet · Noise Robust 4 · Analyse · stack best 30–50% · drizzle 3.0× · RGB Align on · 16-bit TIFF.
  5. RegiStax 6: RGB Align (if fringes) · Wavelet layers 2–3 + denoise 0.10–0.30 · Do All · save to fotos\planeten\gestackt\.
  6. GIMP: Levels · Color Balance (warm yellow) · Unsharp Mask 0.5/1.5 px · export JPEG → fotos\planeten\bewerkt\saturnus-2026-11-17.jpg. A tiny oval with a ring — from 300 mm. 🪐

Recipe C — Jupiter with the telescope (target: 30 minutes of processing)

  1. Telescope set up outside 60–90 minutes in advance, polar-aligned, target centred, SV705C at prime focus with the UV/IR-cut filter (see the Telescope page).
  2. SharpCap: RAW8 + Debayer Preview · Capture Area 640x480 (150–250 fps) · Exposure 5–15 ms · Gain to histogram 60–75% · SER · 90–120 s.
  3. AutoStakkert!4: Planet (COG) · AP 48 px on the planet · Analyse · stack best 20% · RGB Align on · 16-bit TIFF.
  4. RegiStax 6: RGB Balance → Auto · Wavelet layers 2–3 + denoise 0.10–0.30 · Do All · save TIFF.
  5. GIMP: Levels · Color Balance (warm cream) · export JPEG. Cloud belts and the four moons — from your own telescope. 🪐

The golden rules, one last time

Stack before you sharpen · sharpen as the last big step · less is more · always keep the original frames — RAW for the D300, SER for the telescope — you will re-stack them better in a year.
