My Collection

Every collection has its stories. Mine includes one copy that most collectors will never hold in their hands.

These are my own copies, documented the way a collector documents them: what they are, why they matter, and how they ended up with me. Photos of the physical books are being added — this page uses only photos of my own collection, never scans of the artwork.

Objectif Lune — the early B8 state

The pride of my collection: a French first-edition Objectif Lune (1953) in an early B8 state, printed around September 1953 — while On a marché sur la Lune was still being serialised in Tintin magazine, and before Casterman started adding promotional text to the back cover.

Its fingerprints:

  • Red cloth spine — the deluxe early cloth finish
  • Back-cover Tintin title list ending at Objectif Lune, with no promotional line "En préparation: On a marché sur la Lune"
  • The Jo, Zette & Jocko list shows 5 titles, ending with Destination Kharamako

Why it matters: this variant is physical evidence of a transition phase in the history of Studios Hergé — as the expert Philippe Goddin describes it, Casterman was already selling advertising space on the back cover while Hergé was recovering from his breakdown and studio staff (notably Bob De Moor) carried the artwork. The copies with the "En préparation" line were printed in far larger numbers throughout 1954 — so this early state is considerably rarer.

Care notes I keep in mind: the 1950s paper contains wood and will brown at the edges; the red cloth spine fades in sunlight; and the cloth binding must not be squeezed tightly between other books. So it lives upright, in the dark, with room to breathe.

The moon albums — reading copies

Besides the collector's pieces, I keep the moon albums as digital reading copies — Destination Moon and Explorers on the Moon, the two albums that tie this whole chapter to the Space chapter. Reading them back-to-back with the real Apollo story is a treat: first Hergé's Moon, then NASA's.
